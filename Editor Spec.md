# Editor Spec

Sep 30, 2026 · Built: an in-engine level editor (Tab) with picking, 2D views, sector tools, placement, undo and saving, and a mesh editor for models. Oct 9: panels and icons with the mouse, sector/face/vertex modes, multi-select, the editor's own keys, turned 2D views, a full-window material editor

## Overview

The editor is a mode of the app, not a separate program: Tab switches between playing and editing, at the same camera, with the real renderer, lighting and shadows. It edits `.mmp` levels (see the Level Format Spec).

**Built:**

- picking with the mouse in the 3D view and in 2D views, by mode (sector, face, vertex; R, F, V), with hover and selection outlines; several things selected at once (Shift, or a box dragged)
- an icon column on the right (add, world, controls, materials, and the modes) and beside it a panel: the active selection's, or an icon's menu, with submenus in the same frame; everything on it is clicked, scrolled or typed into
- entity editing: move, turn, scale, kind, model, animation, static, occluder, duplicate, delete
- sector tools: extrude, push/pull, cleave, merge sectors, open and wall up, new room, delete sector, move and turn sectors, vertex moves, split a face into triangles
- placement: lights (with spot cones), props (by model), spawn points; the level's ambient light, sun and meta values
- texture alignment on surfaces (levels with uvs)
- undo and redo, saving, copy and paste, camera bookmarks, hot reload of models, textures and the level
- the editor's own keys, bound in the gear menu (separate from the game's hotkeys)
- 2D views along the six axes and turned between (isometric views), with the selection dragged on the grid and clicks going through to what's behind
- a full-window material editor (the fourth icon)
- a mesh editor (M on an entity) for `.mmdl` models: shadow proxies, proxy boxes per bone, polygon colors

**Next:**

- materials and levels of detail in the mesh editor
- trying the Blender exporter in Blender (written, but untested there: see the Model Format Spec)
- opening a level that won't load in the editor (today it stops at startup, naming the face or sector: mend it in the file, or don't save it broken)
- per-sector properties once the level format has more than meta values

## How edits work

The editor works on the level's own tables, `moose_assets::LevelDoc`: the `.mmp` file's sections as rows (vertices, attributes and their values, sectors, surfaces, adjoins, entities, ambient, lights, directional).

- **Every edit changes the tables, then the app rebuilds the level from them** (`App::rebuild`): the tables are written as text, loaded by the real level loader, and the world, its lights, baked shadows and cube maps are made again. The camera stays put. A rebuild takes a few milliseconds on the test levels.
- **The loader checks every edit.** If it refuses the new tables (a bent surface, a non-convex sector, an entity outside its sector), the edit is undone and the loader's message is shown, except for moves of geometry, which are kept for the artist to finish (see Tools). The message's line points at the table row it's about (`LevelDoc::row_at_line`), which is outlined in red for a few seconds. This is the editor's live validation: the same rules as loading, with nothing to keep in step.
- **Undo and redo** are snapshots of the tables (Ctrl+Z, Ctrl+Y or Ctrl+Shift+Z).
- **Saving** (Ctrl+S) writes the tables to the level's file. The file is regenerated, so hand comments in it are not kept; the test levels are generated by `tools/gen_test_assets.py` anyway, and a level edited here should not be regenerated after.
- **Selections name table rows** (a surface, sector, vertex, entity or light), so they survive rebuilds. Drawn polygons map back to rows: the level's drawn polygons are its solid surfaces in order, and the world's entities are the entity rows that aren't spawn points, in order.

## Views

- **3D** is the rendered view. F5 draws every edge of the level (openings in magenta) and its entities over it, clipped at the near plane (`wire.rs`).
- **2D views** are wireframes on a grid, as classic level editors show them: 1 to 6 the six along the axes (top, bottom, front, back, left, right), 7 the 3D view; F6 cycles them all.
  - **Turned:** in a 2D view the arrows turn it (left and right about the world's up, up and down toward straight down or up) by the turn step, 6° unless the gear menu says otherwise, for looking at things from an angle: an isometric view is a few presses from the front. A view's orientation is a yaw and pitch (`wire::Axis`); the named ones are those along the axes.
  - **Why not a rendered orthographic view:** the renderer sorts by `w` (1 over the distance), which is the same everywhere in an orthographic view, and inserts world polygons without overlap tests because portal clipping keeps them from overlapping. An orthographic view of the whole level has neither.
  - **The grid** is the world's, on the world plane that faces the view most squarely, through the view's middle (the view's own plane when it looks along an axis; the floor seen from an angle): lines every grid step where they are at least 6 px apart on screen, foreshortening counted (doubling until they are), brighter every meter and along the world axes. Edge-on, there is none.
  - **Moving around:** drag with the right button or use WASD (the fly keys) to pan; the wheel zooms about the pointer.
- **Holding the right button** looks around in the 3D view (the cursor is locked while held); otherwise the cursor is free for pointing. The 3D view flies on the editor's keys: WASD, Space up, Z down (Q and E don't roll here).
- **Top left**, the view, the mode, whether openings are picked in 3D, and unsaved.

## Picking

**Modes** (R, F, V, or the three icons under the menus): what a click picks. Entities and lights are picked in any mode.

- **Sector mode:** a face clicked stands for its sector.
- **Face mode:** faces (openings too, see below).
- **Vertex mode:** the corners of the faces in play: the faces selected when the mode was entered (or a selected sector's), drawn outlined with their corners as dots. A click near a dot picks the vertex; a click on a face makes it the face in play (Shift: one more), so V isn't needed again to change faces. Vertices sharing a place but not a row stay apart this way.
- **Switching modes infers up:** from vertices to faces, the faces in play; from faces or vertices to sectors, their sectors. Otherwise a mode gets back what it had selected when it was left. An entity or light selected stays selected.

**What's under the pointer:**

- **3D view:** the nearest drawn polygon (`ViewGeometry::pick`): a point-in-polygon test on the view's projected polygons, nearest by `w`, which is linear across a projected polygon. Polygons seen in mirrors are skipped. With openings on (O), the nearest opening along the ray is picked if it's before what's drawn (openings aren't drawn).
- **2D views:** everything under the pointer, nearest first: entities whose box on screen holds it, surfaces (openings too) it is inside on screen or within 7 px of an edge of (one seen edge-on is a line). Nearest is where the line of sight through the pointer meets each; at one depth (an opening's two sides), the one facing the view first (`Editor::pick_2d_all`). **Clicking again in the same place picks the next one down**, until 3 s pass without a click or the pointer moves; what the next click would pick is outlined as the pointer's.
- **Markers:** lights (a diamond, with a line where a spot light shines) and spawn points (a square, with a line where they face) are picked by clicking within a few pixels, in either view. The selected light's range is a circle in a 2D view.

**Selection:** an active one (the last picked; its panel shows, outlined brightest) and the rest selected with it (`Editor::multi`, outlined dimmer).

- **Click:** that alone. **Shift+click:** it joins the selection as the active one, or leaves it if it was in it.
- **A box** dragged with the left button from where nothing selected is: what's in it is selected when it's let go (Shift: added to what was). By mode: the corners that can be picked, the faces whose middles are inside it, or the sectors whose middles or one of whose faces' middles are; in the 3D view only those drawn this frame (seen).
- **Dragging the selection** (2D views): pressing on anything selected and dragging moves the whole selection along the screen's axes, on the grid, live; one undo takes the drag back, Esc puts it back. Let go without moving, it's a click.

## Tools

**The icons** (right edge, top down):

- **+ Add:** a new room (4 × 3 × 4 m where the view is), a light, a prop (then its model, from a searchable list), a spawn point.
- **World:** the level's stats, its ambient light and sun (typed or scrolled; with a day and night, where it is at noon), the sun's day and night and clouds (how long a day lasts, when it starts, in hours, the clouds' seed, the weather, the wind and where it comes from; see the Lighting Spec), Sky faces: day and night sky (gives the sky faces `sky_cycle`), Add a sun for a level without one, its meta values, and **Find by number** (also `/`): `sector 3`, `face 12` (or `f12`), `vertex 7`, `light 2`, or an entity's name selects it (in its mode) and brings the view to it: a 2D view centered on it, the 3D view backed off from it along where it looks (or, outside the level, turned to it). While the tables won't load, **Go to the problem** does the same for what the loader refused.
- **Gear:** the editor's controls (see Keys), and the 2D views' turn step.
- **Materials** (a square of dithered gradient): the material editor, over the whole window (see below).

**The panel** beside them shows the active selection (or, with none, "No face selected" in the mode). Its top lines are stats, in smaller type and their own color: what the selection is, not what to change. Rows below are clicked (an action, or a choice to open), scrolled (a number steps), or clicked to type a number (Enter sets it, Esc drops it; positions, sizes, angles, lights' and the level's numbers, extrude and push distances, texture numbers). A submenu (the lights kept off, meta values, the material browser, Add's props) opens in the same frame with **< Back** at the top left; Esc goes back too. Paired texture rows sit two to a line.

**Lists** (lights, materials, models, controls) scroll with the wheel, and with Up/Down (the keys' row is highlighted) and Enter while the pointer is over the panel. **Searches** filter as they're typed.

**Deleting** (the Delete row, X, or Del) asks first: Yes or No, clicked, or Left/Right and Enter (Esc: no).

| Selection | Rows |
| --- | --- |
| Nothing | The mode's "No sector selected" (the icons hold the rest) |
| Surface | Its sector (click: that sector, in sector mode), and for a solid surface: material (the browser: a search, its textures previewed in slot order under the list, Apply at the top gives the selected faces the one clicked), stitch from a touching face, merge with touching faces (M; several selected: the ones that merge), cut (C), reflective and sky, extrude (E; its depth typed or scrolled), push/pull (typed or scrolled, or PgUp/PgDn), open to a match (J; with two faces selected, those two), split into triangles, lights kept off (below), meta values, texture rows; for an opening: the sector it's in, wall it up (both sides), meta values |
| Sector | Cleave (C, then the cut), merge (M: with one selected, then click a face of the other; with several, the ones that merge), its middle X, Y, Z (typed or scrolled: moves it) and turn (scroll, or `[` `]`), lights kept off (below), meta values, delete (X) |
| Vertex | X, Y, Z (typed or scrolled; also the arrows and PgUp/PgDn); several selected move together |
| Entity | Kind, model (cycles through assets/models), animation (for a model with them: its animations, or none), position, yaw/pitch/roll, scale, static, occluder (mesh or none), lights kept off (drawn entities), duplicate, delete |
| Light | Position, color, range, source radius, shadows, spot on/off with aim and cone angles, duplicate, delete |

**Sector tools** are `LevelDoc` methods in `moose_assets` (`mmp_edit.rs`), each tested by loading its result:

- **Extrude:** a surface becomes an opening, and a prism behind it becomes a new sector: its far end (selected after, to extrude again) and a side per edge take the surface's attribute values.
- **Push/pull:** a surface's corners move along its normal; surfaces sharing them stretch.
- **Cleave** (C, then the cut in a 2D view: dragged, or its two ends clicked, on the grid or with Ctrl/Cmd held on the nearest vertex; C again or Esc stops): a sector is split by a plane (through the cut, square to the view, which may be turned) into two sectors joined by an opening; with several selected, every one the plane crosses is, each into two. Surfaces crossing the plane are split, with one new vertex per crossed edge and attribute values between the edge's ends. Openings to other sectors that the plane crosses are split on both sides. New vertices on other surfaces' edges are added to them as corners, so there are no T-junctions. Entities and lights behind the plane move to the new sector.
- **Merge with a sector:** then click any face of the other sector, or an opening of the selected one onto it (Esc stops). The two must open onto each other and be convex together (a room and a hallway through a doorway aren't: the wall around the door would be inside); otherwise it is refused and says which. The openings between them go, and the other's surfaces, entities and lights become the selected sector's, which keeps its name and meta values; the sectors after the other move down one. Where the openings were, two surfaces on one plane that look alike (the same material, flags and options, and the texture running on unchanged) become one, two openings onto the same sector become one on both sides, and corners left in a straight line there go where every surface using them allows. So a cleave merged back is as it was (`LevelDoc::merge_sectors`). One undo takes it back.
- **Lights kept off:** a solid surface's, a sector's and a drawn entity's panels open a list of the lights that can reach them (within their range of it; for a surface, in front of it), nearest first, then the flashlight and the sun: checked is kept off (`exclude_lights=`; an entity's from its template marked so). Clicking one (or Enter on the keys' row) checks or unchecks it, for everything selected like the active one; a light without a name is named for it (`light`, `light_1`, ...), the sun `sun`. The row before it says which are kept off.
- **Meta values** (a sector's, a surface's, a vertex's, the level's in World): `$KEY=VALUE` rows, the key and the value each clicked to type (checked as the loader does: a key is letters, digits and `_`; a value one or more numbers), an x to remove one, and Add a value.
- **Merge several sectors** (M with several selected): any two of them that open onto each other and are convex together merge, over and over while two do, so a grid of cleaved pieces comes back whole whatever the order (`merge_pairs`); the active one keeps its name. **Merge several faces** works the same way.
- **Move and turn sectors:** the arrows, PgUp/PgDn, the middle's typed X, Y, Z, a drag in 2D, and `[` `]` (15°) move or turn every selected sector with its corners, and with them the entities and lights in it. Corners other sectors share move too, so connected sectors stay joined.
- **Geometry moves the level won't load are kept** (`Field::moves_geometry`: sectors', faces' and vertices' moves, turns, pushes): the editor keeps the tables and draws the level as it last loaded, and the bottom line says why in red with what by number (`face 40 of sector 3: polygon is not planar`), outlined in red; World has Go to the problem. Every edit is kept while they don't load (the loader can't check them), and the first that loads clears it; undo goes back as ever. Saving them like this asks first: a level that won't load stops the app at startup (saying which face or sector by number).
- **Vertex meta:** a vertex's panel opens its meta values (for materials reading `vertex:KEY`, blended across its faces). A face's panel warns (in orange, under its stats) where its corners don't all have a key, or have it with different counts of numbers: those corners use the material's fallback. Cuts carry it on: a vertex a cut makes on an edge (cutting a face, or cleaving) gets each meta value both ends have, blended by how far along it is (each number whole where both ends' are), so what a material reads across the face stays as it was; extruding copies a vertex's to the vertex made from it.
- **Weld** (a vertex's panel): **Weld nearby** merges every vertex surfaces use within a grid step of the active one into it (at its place); with several selected, **Weld the selected into this one** merges them. Every face's corner at one of them is at it then, corners that come together in a face are one, and a face left without area (a sliver between them) goes; an opening that would is refused (`LevelDoc::weld_vertices`). For a cut that missed a corner by a little, which leaves near-duplicate corners that keep faces from merging (their union isn't quite convex). One undo takes it back.
- **Vary tint** (a face's panel; the selected faces): `$tint` on their vertices, each a little darker or lighter, warmer or cooler (seeded), white (255,255,255) on vertices a face carrying them on shares (one facing the same way: a puddle in the dirt), so the painted faces meet it with no seam; vertices only walls share are painted (a corner shows no seam) (`LevelDoc::vary_vertex_meta`). For `colored_bumpy` with `tint vertex:tint|255,255,255`.
- **Split into triangles** (a face's panel; several selected: each): ear clipping as seen square on (along its corners' summed normal), so a bent or dented face becomes flat convex triangles, each keeping its winding, options, flags and corners' values (`LevelDoc::triangulate_surface`). Merge them back where they're flat. An entity keeps a list of its own only where it differs from its template's. Deleting a light takes it off every list; duplicating one leaves the copy unnamed. The Light panel shows a light's name.
- **Open to a match:** a surface and another sector's surface with corners in the same places become an opening pair; their corners are welded into the same vertices.
- **Wall up:** an opening pair becomes solid again, taking attribute values from the sector's other surfaces at the same vertices.
- **New room:** a box sector on its own, with each attribute's first values. Open it to the level with a matching wall, or build it by extruding instead.
- **New level** (Esc > Levels, not the editor's panel): a name is typed, and a level of one 10 × 5 × 10 m room starts, not saved yet: every surface the `default` material, textured flat (a tile every 2 m), a spawn point looking down the room, a light under the ceiling and some ambient light. Levels > Save level (or Ctrl+S here) writes it to `assets/levels/NAME.mmp`; until then the editor counts it as unsaved.
- **Delete sector:** openings into it are walled up; refused while an entity or light is in it.
- **Vertices** move on the grid; the loader refuses a move that bends a surface or dents a sector.

**Cut a surface** (a solid surface selected, in a 2D view): press C, then drag a line across it (or click its two ends); letting go cuts it (and every other selected solid face the plane crosses) in two along the plane through the line, square to the view (the whole surface, wherever the line crosses it). Ends snap to the grid, or with Ctrl (Cmd) held, to the nearest vertex on screen. Nothing else is split: no new sector or opening. The halves keep the surface's material, flags and options, and their texture coordinates run on from its own, so it looks the same. The new corners go into every surface sharing the cut edges (the floor and ceiling under a cut wall, and both sides of an opening the cut lands on), so there are no T-junctions. C again or Esc stops a cut; one undo takes it back. Refused for openings and lines that miss the surface.

**Merge with a touching face** (a solid surface's panel): then click a surface to merge into it (Esc stops). The two must be in the same sector, on one plane facing the same way, and touch along one run of edges, and their union must be convex (corners in a straight line are fine); otherwise it is refused and says which. The selected surface becomes the union: its material, flags and options, and with a `uv` attribute its texture mapping (fitted to its corners) carried across the whole of it, so the texture runs on with no seam. Corners left in a straight line along its edges stay where another surface uses them (the floor and ceiling under a cut wall: no T-junction) and go otherwise. One undo takes it back. The undoing of **Cut a surface**, and the way to tidy a wall built from pieces.

**Stitch from a touching face** (a solid surface's panel): then click a surface that shares an edge with it (Esc stops). The selected surface takes the clicked one's material (`material=` and `filterN=`; its own meta values stay) and, with a `uv` attribute, its texture mapping unfolded about the shared edge: the texture runs on across the fold as if the two were one flat surface, no seam on the edge and no stretching (the mapping is fitted to the clicked surface's corners, so it follows however that one was aligned). A coplanar neighbor simply continues. **Right-click** the surface instead for a mirrored stitch: the texture reflected across the shared edge (folded back onto the clicked surface's side, so a point some way past the edge takes the texture as far back from it), still with no seam on the edge. While a surface is being picked, the right button is this click rather than a look around. Refused for openings and surfaces that don't share an edge; one undo takes it back.

**Texture alignment** (levels with a `uv` attribute): shift across and down by 1/8 of a tile, scale about the surface's middle, size across or down, turn, flip across or down (mirrored about the middle: left to right, or top to bottom), or map flat from the corners' positions exactly as the test levels are made (along the normal's strongest axis, a tile every 2 m, v growing down on walls). New values go in new rows, so surfaces sharing rows keep theirs. Scrolling steps by 1/8 of a repeat, a quarter of the scale and 15°; with Alt (Option) held, by 1/128, a hundredth and 1° (Alt, not Shift: macOS turns Shift+wheel into a sideways scroll).

The panel shows the surface's mapping as numbers, fitted to its corners' coordinates: **across** and **down** (its offset, in repeats), **size across** and **size down** (meters a repeat; negative is flipped) and **turn** (degrees), all relative to the flat mapping (offset 0, 2 m, 0°; a shear, as stitching can leave, is kept but not shown). Clicking one types a number into it: Enter sets it (a unit after it, `m` or `°`, is let be), Esc drops it; while typing, the keys are the text's and the view holds still. Sizes and turns keep the texture at the surface's middle where it was; offsets are set as typed. A surface whose coordinates aren't one flat mapping (all in a line) shows `scroll` and takes no typing.

Each works in a context: the editor's everywhere, the others with a sector, face, entity or light selected, or in the mesh editor, so one key can do one thing per context (C cleaves a sector and cuts a face). The gear menu lists them all by context: clicking one waits for its new key; a key another action of the same context (or the editor's) had swaps to that one's old key. They're kept in `~/.config/moose/editor_keys.txt` with the 2D turn step.

## Material editor

The fourth icon opens it over the whole window, on the selected face's material if it has one (`material_screen.rs`, over `material_edit`'s draft):

- **Left:** the materials, with a search and New material; one clicked opens.
- **Right:** its rows (as the Material Format Spec has them: name, shader, each texture slot and its sampler, each input's source, value and fallback, save). Under the shader and under each input, what it does: shader programs and their inputs carry a description in the registry (`ShaderInfo::about`, `Input::about`). Clicking a row types it (the name, a value), steps it (a sampler, a source: right click or the wheel the other way, Left/Right with the keys), or opens its picker.
- **The shader's picker:** a searchable list of the programs; one clicked shows its description, texture slots and inputs (each with what it does) beside the list; Select takes it.
- **A texture's picker:** a searchable list of what a slot can be (the texture files, cube maps, the engine's own); one clicked shows it beside the list; Select takes it (none empties the slot).
- **Keys:** Up/Down and Enter in the rows or the picker's list, Left/Right step a row, Esc leaves a picker, then the editor. `< Editor` at the top leaves too.

## Mesh editor

M (or the entity's "Mesh editor" row) opens the selected entity's `.mmdl` model, shown on that entity (posed, if it's animated). It sets what Blender doesn't know (see the Model Format Spec):

- **Picking:** a click picks one of the model's drawn polygons in the 3D view (the renderer's picking). With P, it picks its shadow proxies instead, which aren't drawn. They are outlined in pink, and a click picks the nearest one facing the eye. The Polygon row steps through all of them.
- **A polygon:** drawn or a shadow proxy; its color (red, green, blue in sixteenths, as a new `color` row so polygons sharing rows keep theirs); delete it.
- **Proxies per bone:** a proxy box around the drawn polygons of a bone (the selected polygon's bone, or the whole model), with that bone; or remove a bone's proxies (all, with nothing selected).
- **How edits work:** as the level's. The model's tables (`moose_assets::ModelDoc`) are written as text, loaded by the model loader (which checks them) into the same asset, and the level is rebuilt so the entities using it (and their posed copies) take it. A refused edit is undone with the loader's message.
- **Undo, redo and saving** (Ctrl+Z, Ctrl+Y, Ctrl+S) are the model's own while the mesh editor is open. Ctrl+S writes the model's file; comments in it aren't kept.
- **Leaving** (M, Esc with no polygon selected, or Done) with unsaved changes is refused once, with a message. Leaving again drops them: the model is read from its file again.

## Display

The editor needed a new display layer: `moose-present` now uses winit (window and input) and softbuffer (pixels on screen) instead of minifb, keeping the app's polling loop through winit's pump events. It adds real fullscreen (Alt+Enter, which is Option+Return on a Mac; also F11 where the system leaves it alone; `--fullscreen`), a locked hidden cursor with raw mouse motion for looking, the cursor's position in framebuffer pixels, clicks, the wheel and typed text. The framebuffer is scaled to fill the window, keeping its shape (high-density displays, resized windows, fullscreen), nearest pixel.

- **On macOS, the compositor does the scaling, on the GPU:** frames go over at the framebuffer's size, and the window is black around them. The window also gets the color space softbuffer tags frames with (device RGB). Without it, macOS converted every pixel of every frame to the display's color profile on the CPU, about 11 ms a frame in a 2560 × 1440 window, which held the game near 80 fps whatever the render time. Showing a 1280 × 720 frame now takes about 1 ms.
- **Elsewhere, the frame is scaled on the CPU,** across threads by rows.
- **The frame rate cap keeps to a schedule,** so it holds exactly.
- **Measuring:** the debug HUD (F3) shows the present time, and `cargo run --release -p moose-present --example present_bench` times presenting alone.

## Known limits

- Every rebuild adds a new level geometry mesh to the asset store (the old one stays, unused): a few kilobytes per edit on the test levels. Cube map textures are reused.
- Extrude copies the surface's attribute values onto the new sector's surfaces, so textures stretch along the new sides: map them flat afterwards.
- Openings are picked in 3D only with O on (they aren't drawn there); in 2D views always.
- Mirror balls' cube maps are re-baked on every rebuild (a few ms each).
- The mesh editor can't move positions or make polygons: shapes come from Blender. Level edits wait while it is open.
- A model file changed on disk while the mesh editor has it open is reloaded under it; the editor's tables win at the next edit or save.

## Models and animation

Agreed Sep 29 (see the editor-and-models direction in memory); built Sep 30 (the Model Format Spec has the details):

- **Blender stays the authoring tool.** A Blender exporter add-on writes our own model format, which keeps convex n-gons (glTF only stores triangles).
- **Skeletal animation, Half-Life style:** one bone per vertex first, with room for up to four weights for linear blend skinning later. Per bone per frame: sample the keyframes (quaternion slerp or nlerp), walk the hierarchy, multiply by the inverse bind pose; per vertex, one matrix multiply on the mesh's position list. Polygons spanning bones bend, so the exporter triangulates only those.
- **Shadow proxies** are one rigid proxy per bone.
- **A mesh editor** in the engine sets what Blender doesn't know: polygon flags, materials, proxies and levels of detail.
